01 · Quality

How to read a COA

A certificate of analysis (COA) is a lab report for one specific batch. These are the parts worth checking on any supplier’s COA.

IdentityMass spectrometry (MS)

Confirms the molecule is what the label says by matching its measured molecular weight to the expected weight. A purity number means little without it.

PurityHPLC purity %

Shows how much of the sample is the target peptide versus other material, usually as a percentage from the main peak on the chromatogram.

TraceabilityBatch or lot number

The lot number on the COA should match the batch you received. A COA with no lot number can’t be tied to a specific vial.

SourceLab name and test date

Look for who ran the test and when. Recent dates and a named lab, ideally independent, make the report easier to trust.

ContentAmount per vial

Some reports also measure how much peptide is actually in the vial, so you can compare it with the amount on the label.

AppearanceForm and color

Lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptides are normally a white or off-white powder or cake. The COA usually notes this.

COAs are available upon request. Email your order number or the product and dose you’re looking at, and I’ll send what’s available for that batch.

02 · Lab practice

Storage & handling

General lab guidance for synthetic peptides, from published handling guides. Every vial ships as a powder.

PowderKeep sealed vials cold

Freeze-dried peptides keep best cold, dry and sealed. Lab guides recommend a freezer (−20 °C or colder) for longer storage.

OpeningLet it warm up first

Let a cold vial reach room temperature before opening it, so moisture from the air doesn’t condense inside.

EnvironmentAvoid light and moisture

Store vials in the dark and keep them dry. Peptide powder absorbs moisture, which shortens its shelf life.

In solutionFridge, and use promptly

Once a peptide is in solution it’s far less stable than the powder. Guides suggest up to about a week at 4 °C, or freezing in small portions.

Freeze–thawAvoid repeated cycles

Each freeze–thaw cycle can degrade a peptide. Splitting a solution into single-use portions avoids thawing the same vial over and over.

LabelsTrack every vial

Note the product, lot number and the date a vial was opened or mixed, so results can be traced back to a batch.

  • Sources: Sigma-Aldrich, Handling and Storage of Peptides · JPT Peptide Technologies, How to Store Peptides

Published research

What has actually been studied, with links to the original papers and trial records. Evidence ranges from FDA-approved drugs to early animal work, so each card is labeled honestly.

NVL-R3Retatrutide · GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist
Human trials

A 48-week phase 2 trial in 338 adults with obesity, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, reported mean weight reductions of up to 24.2%. In May 2026, Lilly reported topline results from its first phase 3 trial, TRIUMPH-1. As of that report, retatrutide was still an investigational drug.

  • Jastreboff et al., Triple–Hormone-Receptor Agonist Retatrutide for Obesity, NEJM 2023
  • TRIUMPH-1 trial record, NCT05929066 · topline May 2026
NVL-T2Tirzepatide · GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist
Approved drug

The SURMOUNT-1 trial followed 2,539 adults with obesity for 72 weeks and reported mean weight reductions of up to 20.9% at the highest dose. The same compound is FDA-approved as Mounjaro and Zepbound.

  • Jastreboff et al., Tirzepatide Once Weekly for the Treatment of Obesity, NEJM 2022
  • SURMOUNT-1 trial record, NCT04184622
TesamorelinGrowth hormone–releasing hormone (GHRH) analog
Approved drug

A 26-week randomized trial of 412 adults with HIV-associated abdominal fat reported about a 15% reduction in visceral (deep belly) fat versus placebo. Tesamorelin is FDA-approved as Egrifta for that condition.

  • Falutz et al., Metabolic Effects of a Growth Hormone–Releasing Factor in Patients with HIV, NEJM 2007
SS-31Elamipretide · mitochondria-targeting peptide
Approved drug

In September 2025, the FDA granted elamipretide (Forzinity) accelerated approval as the first treatment for Barth syndrome, a rare mitochondrial disease. It acts on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

  • HealthDay, FDA grants accelerated approval for first treatment for Barth syndrome, 2025
MOTS-cMitochondria-derived peptide
Early / animal

The 2015 paper that described MOTS-c found that, in mice, it improved insulin sensitivity and prevented diet-induced obesity. Human research is still early.

  • Lee et al., The mitochondrial-derived peptide MOTS-c promotes metabolic homeostasis, Cell Metabolism 2015
IpamorelinSelective growth hormone secretagogue
Early / animal

First described in 1998 as a growth hormone secretagogue that releases growth hormone selectively, without the rise in ACTH and cortisol seen with earlier compounds in animal studies.

  • Raun et al., Ipamorelin, the first selective growth hormone secretagogue, Eur J Endocrinol 1998
BPC-157Synthetic pentadecapeptide
Early / animal

A 2025 systematic review found the evidence is almost entirely from animal studies of tendon, ligament, muscle and bone healing. The only human study it found was small, with 12 patients, and the authors note that human safety is still unknown.

  • Vasireddi et al., Emerging Use of BPC-157 in Orthopaedic Sports Medicine: A Systematic Review, HSS Journal 2025
GHK-CuCopper-binding tripeptide
Early / lab & skin

A naturally occurring peptide studied in wound-healing and skin research. A 2018 review summarizes lab data showing it influences the activity of many genes involved in tissue repair.

  • Pickart & Margolina, Regenerative and Protective Actions of the GHK-Cu Peptide, Int J Mol Sci 2018
