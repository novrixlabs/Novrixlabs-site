01 · Basics

Peptides 101

Three ideas explain most of what you’ll read about peptides.

🔗
Short chains of amino acids

Amino acids are the building blocks of proteins. Link a few together and you have a peptide. Glutathione is just 3 of them, BPC-157 is 15. Past roughly 50 you’re usually talking about a protein, like growth hormone at 191.

🔑
Keys and locks

Most peptides work by fitting a receptor on a cell, like a key in a lock. An “agonist” switches the receptor on. A dual or triple agonist, like tirzepatide or retatrutide, fits two or three different locks at once.

❄️
Why it comes as a powder

Peptides break down fairly quickly in water, so they’re freeze-dried (lyophilized) into a powder for shipping and storage. That’s why vials arrive as a small white cake.

02 · History

A century of peptides

From a Toronto lab in 1921 to the trials making headlines today.

1921–22Insulin changes everything

Banting and Best begin experiments in Toronto in 1921. In January 1922, 14-year-old Leonard Thompson becomes the first person treated with purified insulin, a peptide hormone.

University of Toronto, Insulin 100
1953The first peptide hormone made in a lab

Vincent du Vigneaud synthesizes oxytocin, a 9-amino-acid hormone, and wins the 1955 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for it.

NobelPrize.org
1963Building peptides on beads

Bruce Merrifield invents solid-phase peptide synthesis, building chains one amino acid at a time on tiny resin beads. It’s still the basis of how most peptides are made, and it won the 1984 Nobel Prize.

NobelPrize.org
1972–76A peptide test you’ve heard of

NIH researchers publish a precise test for the hormone hCG in 1972. It becomes the basis of modern pregnancy tests, and the first US home test arrives in 1976.

NIH Office of History
1973GHK found in human blood

Loren Pickart discovers the copper-binding peptide GHK in human plasma, starting decades of skin and wound-healing research.

Pickart & Margolina, IJMS 2018
1985Growth hormone from bacteria

The FDA approves the first recombinant (lab-made) growth hormone, from Genentech, replacing hormone extracted from human pituitary glands.

Genentech
1992 → 2005The Gila monster drug

VA endocrinologist John Eng finds exendin-4 in Gila monster venom. It becomes exenatide (Byetta), FDA-approved in 2005 as the first GLP-1 drug.

US Dept. of Veterans Affairs
2017–23The GLP-1 era

Semaglutide (Ozempic) is approved in 2017. Tirzepatide, the first dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, follows as Mounjaro in 2022 and Zepbound in 2023.

Eli Lilly
2025A mitochondria peptide is approved

The FDA grants elamipretide (SS-31) accelerated approval as the first treatment for Barth syndrome, a rare mitochondrial disease.

HealthDay
2026Triple agonists reach phase 3

Lilly reports topline results from TRIUMPH-1, the first phase 3 trial of retatrutide: average weight loss of up to 25% at 80 weeks, versus 3.9% on placebo.

The Pharmaceutical Journal

07 · Quality

How to read a COA

A certificate of analysis (COA) is a lab report for one specific batch. These are the parts worth checking on any supplier’s COA.

IdentityMass spectrometry (MS)

Confirms the molecule is what the label says by matching its measured molecular weight to the expected weight. A purity number means little without it.

PurityHPLC purity %

Shows how much of the sample is the target peptide versus other material, usually as a percentage from the main peak on the chromatogram.

TraceabilityBatch or lot number

The lot number on the COA should match the batch you received. A COA with no lot number can’t be tied to a specific vial.

SourceLab name and test date

Look for who ran the test and when. Recent dates and a named lab, ideally independent, make the report easier to trust.

ContentAmount per vial

Some reports also measure how much peptide is actually in the vial, so you can compare it with the amount on the label.

AppearanceForm and color

Lyophilized peptides are normally a white or off-white powder or cake. The COA usually notes this.

08 · Lab practice

Storage & handling

General lab guidance for synthetic peptides, from published handling guides.

PowderKeep sealed vials cold

Freeze-dried peptides keep best cold, dry and sealed. Lab guides recommend a freezer (−20 °C or colder) for longer storage.

OpeningLet it warm up first

Let a cold vial reach room temperature before opening it, so moisture from the air doesn’t condense inside.

EnvironmentAvoid light and moisture

Store vials in the dark and keep them dry. Peptide powder absorbs moisture, which shortens its shelf life.

In solutionFridge, and use promptly

Once a peptide is in solution it’s far less stable than the powder. Guides suggest up to about a week at 4 °C, or freezing in small portions.

Freeze–thawAvoid repeated cycles

Each freeze–thaw cycle can degrade a peptide. Splitting a solution into single-use portions avoids thawing the same vial over and over.

LabelsTrack every vial

Note the product, lot number and the date a vial was opened or mixed, so results can be traced back to a batch.
